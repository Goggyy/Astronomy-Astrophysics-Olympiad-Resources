# Suggested Topics for beginner-intermediate IOAA Preparation

Through various textbooks and my own notes I've looked at while preparing for the IOAA myself, I’ve identified some of the most useful topics for IOAA preparation. The breakdown below covers most astronomy and astrophysics competition topics at a beginner-to-intermediate level. Not all formulas and topics in the IOAA are covered here; it focuses on the most common, highest-yield, and most appropriate topics for beginners. Niche and less frequently tested topics are left out. I believe this set of topics would be useful for students who starts with little prior experience with astrophysics. 

### Proposed handout format

Each handout would reference a textbook section for detailed explanation, followed by a concise summary of all concepts and formulas, and past paper problems with hints. This make the resources more accessible to beginners.

These are suggestions rather than a fixed curriculum, and the topic selection and order may change as resources are developed.

## Practical

### Observation — Introduction

- Introduction to alt-az and equatorial coordinate systems
- Time: Sidereal time, civil time, sidereal year, tropical year
- Motion of the solar system

### Observation — Star Maps

- Recognising constellations and asterisms: Most zodiac constellations, identifying north/south poles, spring/autumnal equinox
- Estimating latitude
- Estimating month or LST using zodiac constellations
- Advanced: More constellations, Bayer designation, Messier objects

### Observation — Telescopes

- Estimating angular size and magnitudes
- Basic telescope use
- Telescope field of view and image orientation

### Data Analysis — Uncertainties in Data

- Uncertainty, significant figures, standard deviations
- Error propagation
- Experimental sources of error
- Finding uncertainties in a data set

### Data Analysis — Graphs

- Plotting and reading from graphs
- Linear and log graph axes
- Line of best fit
- Linearising functions

### Spherical Geometry

- Cartesian and spherical coordinate systems (alt-az and equatorial coordinate systems)
- Spherical trigonometry
- Astronomical triangle
- Distances between points on a sphere

## Theory

There are lots of excellent handouts online covering the following topics too.

### Celestial Mechanics

- Orbital formulas, e.g. Kepler’s laws
- Two-body systems
- Advanced: Galaxy rotation, dark matter
- Advanced: Newton shell, impact parameters

### Stars

- Stefan–Boltzmann, Wien’s displacement, etc.
- Flux, luminosity
- Scaling laws in relation to mass
- Basic stellar evolution
- Compact objects
- Advanced: Nuclear physics, hydrostatic equilibrium, virial theorem

### Telescopes and Detectors

- Telescope optics
- Magnification, field of view, resolving power
- CCD detectors

### Radiation and Photometry

- Magnitudes and distances, extinction
- Parallax
- Spectroscopy: Galaxy rotation speed, velocity dispersion, Doppler effect, etc.

### Light Curves

- Binary-system transits
- Exoplanets
- Variable stars

### Cosmology

- Redshift, Doppler, Hubble law
- Advanced: Friedmann equation, geometry of the universe

## Mathematics at the IOAA

The IOAA also assumes contestants have a set of maths skills. It may be useful to cover the following topics during training, especially for students who haven’t come across them in the school curriculum.

- Calculator skills: Numerical solutions (approximations) to equations
- Ellipse geometry (for orbits)
- Logarithms and exponentials
- Basic trigonometry
- Small angle approximations, binomial approximations
- Dimensional analysis
- Advanced: Vectors, dot products, and cross products (useful if students use vectors instead of spherical geometry)
- Advanced: Basic calculus, differentiation and integration
